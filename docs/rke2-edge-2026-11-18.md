# Combined Rancher, RKE2 and edge lab, 18 November 2026

The published CLO integrations completed one synthetic combined lab: Rancher
provisioned three private RKE2 nodes, and the released edge controller fenced a
failed active edge and moved its public IPv4 to an initially private-only standby.
**Production acceptance remains pending.** [Sanitized measurements](rke2-edge-2026-11-18.json).

## Topology and method

Rancher 2.15.3, Node Driver 0.1.0 and UI Extension 0.1.6 were already installed on
permanent management (2 shared vCPU / 8 GB). Native Rancher provisioning scaled
one to two to three all-role RKE2 v1.36.5+rke2r1 nodes, each 2 shared vCPU / 4 GB /
20 GB boot, with no public address. A project NAT supplied outbound access.
The GUI form workflow was not repeated in this run.

Two dedicated HAProxy VMs and an independent private controller VM each used
1 shared vCPU / 2 GB / 10 GB boot. Traefik 3.7.19 ran on two RKE2 nodes; the third
provided etcd quorum and a synthetic fixture. HAProxy sent PROXYv2 to private
Traefik, which trusted only the two edge addresses and sanitized forwarded headers.
A private lab CA and synthetic hostname were verified on all TLS clients.
No production DNS, data or telephony was changed; CSI was not installed.

The exact v0.1.0-rc.1 release binary was used, SHA-256
`3707827040f6408b938278ca9b87dec7c917a2680b6ef0e488bcd9eb003744ed`.
The packaged systemd service ran as `clo-edge` on its fixed independent VM.
Default detection/convergence policy was retained: 5 s polls, 2 s probes,
30 s suspicion, 180 s per-phase convergence and 600 s re-arm cooldown.

## Observation limits

These are samples, not an SLA or seamless failover. The timed edge observer used
fresh verified TLS connections and HTTP framing, a 5 s budget per I/O step and no
sample retries. Its 40 samples included 28 failures; the first successful request
started 122.395 s after injection. Sampling, SSH command latency, cloud
convergence and cross-host clock uncertainty affect the figures. A first successful
response does not prove sustained availability: later public-probe failures also
occurred, and their network/provider cause was not established.

The first node-loss orchestrator ended with SIGTERM of unknown origin after its
single stop had been accepted. Native state was reconciled before continuing;
the stop was never replayed. Across two observer segments there were 83 samples,
24 timeouts and one certificate-verification failure. The gap and unsuccessful
requests prevent a seamless backend-failover claim. Certificate verification was
never disabled. Functional HTTP/WebSocket setup used bounded transport retries,
recorded privately; those are not outage measurements.

Temporary Prometheus, blackbox exporter and Alertmanager ran as native processes
on the workstation outside CLO. Local webhook receipts proved HTTPS-down firing
and resolution for the shared-ingress and edge faults, and a disarmed-controller
alert after completion. The receiver was an owned loopback test service. Permanent
external monitoring and a human alert recipient remain to be commissioned.
RecoveryRequired and missing-metrics rules were configured but not fault-triggered.
Docker Desktop host networking initially addressed its VM namespace; that invalid
monitor attempt was replaced, and its three owned containers were removed.

Native lost-response injection, controller-host HA, automatic failback, load/soak
qualification, SIP/RTP and production-data migration remain unqualified. Existing
TCP/WebSocket sessions are not migrated. The separate CSI native-volume expansion
NO-GO remains unchanged.

## Cleanup

The controller daemon was stopped and its fixed host deleted before further IP
operations. The exact owned Rancher cluster and machine configuration were deleted
through Rancher; all six temporary VMs and six boot volumes returned 404. The owned
NAT was deleted. Independent native inventory and management health were read back.
Permanent Rancher, its boot/IP, Node Driver, UI Extension and cloud credential remain.
Local monitor processes/listeners were stopped; no owned monitor containers remain.

One newly reserved, free IPv4 remains under CLO's seven-day minimum age. Its earliest
deletion is **25 November 2026, 10:32:05 UTC**, and its listed cost is **150 RUB/month**
until deleted. No automatic deletion schedule was created. Final project counts:
one permanent server, one boot volume, three addresses, no NAT, load balancers or
keypairs. Raw IPs, native resource IDs, credentials, keys and operational logs are
not published. The existing prerelease assets and tags were not replaced.
